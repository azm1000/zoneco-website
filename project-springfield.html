Projects / Springfield, Ohio

A patchwork of decades-old regulations, replaced.

Springfield engaged ZoneCo with a clear mandate: transform its accumulated regulations into a modern, legally defensible, user-friendly framework that advances the city's goals for housing, economic development, and quality of place.

Client
City of Springfield
Adopted
2025
Partners
Urban Fast Forward; CUDA Studio
Population
About 58,000

What we did

The project launched with a diagnostic that mapped every friction point in the existing ordinance: conflicting definitions, discretionary bottlenecks, ambiguous procedures, and standards that no longer reflected Springfield's built form or policy priorities. Paired with stakeholder interviews, the diagnostic gave the city and the drafting team a shared understanding of the problems and of the outcomes residents and businesses wanted most.

The new code reorganizes content into a logical structure with plain-language purpose statements, consolidated use tables, and graphics that explain standards at a glance. Where the prior code relied on scattered overlays and case-by-case relief, the rewrite introduces context-based districts and calibrated form standards for height, frontage, and massing that align with Springfield's traditional neighborhoods and emerging corridors. The code implements the comprehensive plan instead of standing apart from it.

What changed

  • Missing-middle housing legalized across a broader range of small-scale types, with simplified approval paths for context-appropriate infill
  • Parking right-sized, with targeted reductions near transit, shared-parking allowances, and off-site and on-street credits
  • Fewer conditional use triggers, clarified home-occupation rules, and an expedited administrative track for predictable, low-impact proposals
  • Defined decision-maker roles, standardized submittals, transparent objective criteria, and published, enforceable timelines
  • Floodplain, tree, and stormwater provisions integrated with site design, with performance-based options for green infrastructure
  • Tailored standards for historic areas that respect established character while accommodating modern building systems and accessibility

Engagement and implementation

Open houses, small-group workshops, and online surveys translated technical choices, such as height transitions and use permissions, into plain-language tradeoffs, with feedback cycles at each milestone. The project also spanned a difficult period, when the city was the target of national political attention; the community's resilience carried through to adoption.

Implementation included a transition memo, staff training, a digital code format, and checklists. Early metrics, including permit processing time, administrative approvals, and uptake of small-scale housing, provide the feedback loop for future amendments.
