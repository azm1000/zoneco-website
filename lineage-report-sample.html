How the comparison works

Each hallmark is a device that defined New York’s 1916 resolution, from its three overlay maps and street-width height rule to its board of appeals and owner protest petition. We mark each as copied (carried over nearly intact, often in the same words), adapted (same idea, different numbers or mechanics), replaced (the topic is handled by a different device), or absent.

Copied counts fully and adapted counts half. Hallmarks central to New York’s scheme count double, so the percentage reflects how much of the core design survives, not just how many phrases match.

What this report is, and is not

This is a historical and comparative reading of the document you provided. It does not account for amendments not included in the document or for how the code is administered in practice.
