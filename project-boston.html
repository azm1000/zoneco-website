Projects / Boston, Massachusetts

Article 80, rebuilt for the city Boston is becoming.

Since 1996, Article 80 of Boston's zoning code has governed the city's major development review. Three decades of amendments had turned it into a procedural archive. ZoneCo helped turn it back into a tool.

Client
Boston Planning & Development Agency
Partner
Matrix Consulting Group
Scope
Article 80 review and rewrite; impact mitigation and community benefits framework
Population
About 675,000

The challenge

Article 80 established thresholds and procedures for large projects, planned development areas, institutional master plans, and smaller proposals. Over nearly thirty years of incremental amendment it had become complex, inconsistent, and misaligned with Boston's goals for housing, equity, sustainability, and community-driven growth. Boston launched a comprehensive modernization effort with ZoneCo to make it clearer, more transparent, and more effective.

What we did

The project began with a rigorous diagnostic. The team mapped the full lifecycle of development review, from scoping to board vote to post-approval compliance, and identified the friction points: opaque thresholds, inconsistent public notice, overlapping departmental reviews, and unpredictable community mitigation outcomes. In parallel, ZoneCo led broad stakeholder engagement with neighborhood leaders, developers, institutional campuses, and city staff through workshops, surveys, and focus groups. Four guiding principles came out of it: clarity, consistency, inclusion, and responsiveness.

The proposed rewrite reorganized Article 80 into streamlined sections for large projects, small projects, institutional master plans, and planned development areas. Use tables, definitions, and procedures were consolidated. Thresholds were recalibrated; for example, the trigger for Boston Civic Design Commission review moved from 100,000 to 200,000 square feet so that staff and community resources go to the projects with the greatest impact. Public notice was modernized to web-based notification and email alerts while retaining print access. The text emphasizes objective standards, tight timelines, and coordination among departments.

Housing and equity

Boston faces a persistent housing shortage. The modernization aligns review procedures with the city's goals to increase supply, preserve affordability, and address displacement risk. By clarifying review tracks and reducing discretionary paths for projects that meet clear public-benefit requirements, the rewritten article enables more streamlined approvals for housing aligned with city policy. Impact advisory groups and engagement practices were revised to include voices that had been left out.

Implementation

Staff training modules, digital checklists, project-review dashboards, and plain-language guides accompany the new text. Early performance monitoring of permit timelines, applicant and community satisfaction, and consistency of board decisions provides the feedback loop for continuous improvement.

Boston's Article 80 modernization shows how a mature, large-city zoning regime can be recalibrated to serve both development momentum and community priorities. For Boston's next chapter, the updated article is less an obstacle and more a strategic enabler.
