Services / Zoning diagnostics

An inherited code doesn't need defending or demolishing. It needs a diagnosis.

Every ZoneCo engagement starts with a zoning diagnostic: a plain-language report on what your code actually says, how it actually performs, and where it conflicts with what your community actually wants. Before anyone drafts a word.

Request a diagnosticRead example reports

Why start here

The New York City Heights of Buildings Commission spent years documenting actual conditions of light, air, congestion, and land-use conflict before it drafted the 1916 ordinance. The ordinance worked because the tools were fitted to the problems. Then the template got copied. The federal Standard Zoning Enabling Act made it easy, Euclid v. Ambler made it safe, and thousands of communities adopted Manhattan's solution without ever doing Manhattan's homework.

A century later, most American zoning codes are inherited, not designed. Amended one grievance at a time. Administered by staff who have learned to work around them instead of with them. The question for today is not whether your community should have zoning. It is whether anyone, in the last century, has verified that the zoning you have still fits the place you are.

What the diagnostic covers

  • A legal and technical audit of the ordinance: conflicting definitions, duplicative approvals, discretionary bottlenecks, ambiguous procedures, and standards that no longer reflect the built form
  • Process mapping of development review from application to post-approval compliance, with the friction points identified
  • Alignment with the comprehensive plan and adopted policy goals for housing, economic development, resilience, and character
  • Stakeholder interviews with staff, boards, developers, neighborhood leaders, and business owners
  • Defensibility review against current constitutional and state-law standards, including sign regulation and religious land use
  • An equity assessment, where requested, using our Zoning Equity Scorecard
  • Prioritized recommendations and a realistic scope, schedule, and budget for what comes next

What you get

A readable report that a council member, a planning commissioner, and a resident can all follow, with the findings organized by priority. Some communities use it to scope a full rewrite. Others use it to plan a series of targeted amendments. Either way, the drafting that follows is fitted to the problems, not to a template.

Diagnose. Calibrate. Codify. In that order, for a reason.
