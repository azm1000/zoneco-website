Founded on three zoning principles

Every code we write starts here. If a regulation doesn't serve one of these, it doesn't belong in the code.

  1. Zoning should regulate only what needs to be regulated to advance the vision and promote public health, safety, and welfare.
  2. Zoning should respect both existing and desired development patterns.
  3. Zoning should be the implementation of a plan, not a barrier to achieving desired outcomes.

Project spotlight

All projects
Boston, Massachusetts

Article 80 modernization

A ground-up rewrite of the 1996 article that governs Boston's large-project review, reorganized around four principles: clarity, consistency, inclusion, and responsiveness.
Gaithersburg, Maryland

Retool Gaithersburg

The city's first comprehensive zoning rewrite since 1965, adopted in 2024. Winner of the 2025 Maryland Sustainable Growth Award for Best Policy.
Palm Beach, Florida

Once-in-a-generation code rewrite

A 1930 code last overhauled in 1974, rebuilt to protect the town's character while addressing sea-level rise and redevelopment pressure. Context-based sub-areas replaced one-size-fits-all standards.
Vienna, Virginia

First comprehensive overhaul since 1969

Adopted in 2023. Calibrated standards along the Maple Avenue corridor, objective findings in place of discretionary steps, and a code that residents can actually read.
Danville, Virginia

CODE Danville

A full update of a code untouched since 2004, written to implement PLAN Danville, the city's 2024 comprehensive plan, and adopted in 2026 in under twelve months.
Springfield, Ohio

Comprehensive zoning rewrite

Decades of patchwork regulations replaced with context-based districts, right-sized parking, and a clear path for missing-middle housing and infill. Adopted 2025.

Political integrity policy

ZoneCo believes in fair, accessible, and transparent government for all. ZoneCo does not make contributions to any political party, campaign, or candidate. We would rather influence our leaders through the power of big ideas. Read the policy

Although some ZoneCo professionals are also attorneys who may be separately engaged to provide legal representation in states where we are licensed to practice law, ZoneCo is not a law firm. ZoneCo professionals do not provide legal representation or services and are not engaged in the practice of law in any jurisdiction. Engaging ZoneCo does not form an attorney-client relationship and, as such, the protections of the attorney-client relationship do not apply. If you wish to create an attorney-client relationship, you are encouraged to contact an attorney of your choosing.
